# Style Families and Layout Structures

Do not reduce all journal art to aged paper. Select a complete visual fingerprint first, then place it in the requested layout structure. Typography descriptions below define each family's material and compositional behavior, not a mandatory font for every subject. Always route the actual title/body voice through [typography-routing.md](typography-routing.md).

## Visual style families

### 1. Glossy digital thematic scrapbook

Reference: `assets/style-reference-1.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** crisp contemporary digital collage on clean white and saturated color fields; no overall paper yellowing.
- **Age:** modern/pristine.
- **Color:** high-chroma palette sampled from the sources, often with one strong complementary accent; deep blacks and bright whites remain clean.
- **Photos:** sharp rectangular prints, Polaroids, film strips, occasional contour cutouts; clean edges rather than pervasive torn paper.
- **Decorations:** glossy die-cut thematic stickers, pearls, postage graphics, flat pattern strips, punchy illustrated objects.
- **Typography:** energetic digital scale changes and sparing handwritten texture; adapt the main display voice to the subject.
- **Depth:** crisp digital layers with selective shadows; colorful and polished rather than dusty or antique.

Best for travel, hobbies, celebrations, fandoms, pets, or vivid source photography. Dynamically replace the reference's sea motifs with subject-derived motifs.

Must avoid: cream vintage wash, kraft-paper dominance, botanical pressings by default, faded brown palette, universal distressing.

### 2. Handmade vintage fandom collage

Reference: `assets/style-reference-2.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** visibly wrinkled ruled notebook paper.
- **Age:** deliberately vintage and handmade.
- **Color:** muted olive, tan, faded red, washed photography.
- **Photos:** one Polaroid, contour-cut cast/subject figures with tan borders, small printed cards.
- **Decorations:** limited olive masking tape, photocopied blocks, cut magazine ephemera.
- **Typography:** analog handwritten/cut-print material whose era and genre follow the actual subject; factual notes remain readable.
- **Depth:** uneven pasted layers, real wrinkles and analog wear.

Best for films, books, musicians, friend groups, subcultures, and nostalgic collections. This is one of the few families where aging is essential.

### 3. Clean modern image archive

Reference: `assets/style-reference-3.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** clean bright white editorial page over a subtle photographic field; not cream craft paper.
- **Age:** contemporary, quiet, pristine.
- **Color:** neutral black/white with one natural-color hero photograph.
- **Photos:** precise rectangles, black-and-white film sequence, large clean color image; minimal or no shadows.
- **Decorations:** essentially none; hierarchy comes from scale and spacing.
- **Typography:** restrained two-voice hierarchy—theme-matched refined display plus small functional labels; handwriting only when justified.
- **Space:** 35–50 percent white space.

Best for family stories, documentary photography, calm travel, memory archives, and intimate moments.

Must avoid: torn kraft paper, tape clusters, stickers, distressed edges, aged beige wash, scrapbook-object props.

### 4. Fujifilm-inspired Japanese cinematic album

Reference: `assets/style-reference-4.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** photographs themselves fill the page; almost no visible paper.
- **Age:** modern Japanese lifestyle photo book with analog photographic character, not an antique scrapbook.
- **Color:** the defining feature is a unified Fujifilm-inspired Japanese film look: softly compressed highlights, luminous but not clipped skies, cyan/green-biased shadows, warm natural skin, restrained saturation, deep but open blacks, golden sunlight, subtle fine grain, gentle local contrast, and consistent color across mixed source photos.
- **Photos:** hard-edged full-bleed tiles touching page edges; no Polaroids and no torn borders.
- **Decorations:** a small isolated cluster of clean graphic badges on one quiet photo area.
- **Typography:** minimal flat photo-book integration by default. When a substantial title is requested, give it a theme-matched display voice rather than automatically using Japanese lifestyle-magazine lettering.
- **Depth:** flat printed photo book with a strong central metal binding.

Best for cohesive environments, outdoor trips, events, and cinematic sequences. Also expose its photographic treatment as an optional layer that may be applied to families 1, 3, 5, 8, or 9 when the user asks for 富士胶片、日系胶片、Japanese film, or a similar look. When reused, borrow the color/tonal treatment only—not the full-bleed layout or binding.

Must avoid: visible cream background, layered paper scraps, tape, scattered doodles, large title blocks.

### 5. Crisp Japanese editorial magazine

Reference: `assets/style-reference-5.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** pure or near-pure white magazine stock.
- **Age:** contemporary, clean, high-key.
- **Color:** source photographs stay bright and natural; black type anchors the page.
- **Photos:** large environmental rectangles plus clean contour-cut people/objects with no paper-colored sticker border.
- **Decorations:** tiny handwritten arrows or callouts, plus source-derived editorial cutouts such as people, food, vehicles, signs, buildings, flowers, products, or distinctive objects. These are flat layout elements, not stickers with thick outlines.
- **Typography:** oversized theme-matched editorial headline, strong numbered sections, compact captions, and a clear baseline grid. `Japanese editorial` describes the grid discipline, not a universal Japanese headline font.
- **Depth:** mostly flat print design; cutouts may overlap the grid but do not cast heavy scrapbook shadows.
- **Background enrichment:** do not leave broad unused white zones. Build secondary layers from enlarged cropped architecture/sky/water/color fields, borderless object cutouts, small location/product cards, thin caption blocks, image-derived line patterns, and oversized partial photographs that bleed off page edges.
- **Coverage target:** photographs and source-derived cutouts 55–70%; typography, rules, captions, and graphic fragments 20–30%; calm white breathing pockets 8–15%. White is an organizing channel, not the dominant empty content.

Best for city guides, markets, food trails, recommendations, profiles, and explanatory travel features.

Must avoid: ivory notebook, page binding as a focal point, torn map backgrounds, dried flowers, tape on every image. A Fujifilm-inspired photographic treatment is allowed when requested, but it must remain a clean editorial print treatment rather than becoming vintage paper.

### 6. 1990s printed numbered catalog

Reference: `assets/style-reference-6.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** off-white commercial print stock with visible halftone/offset texture.
- **Age:** explicitly retro advertising/catalog print.
- **Color:** forest green, navy, khaki and signal red or dynamically equivalent muted commercial colors.
- **Photos:** people and objects contour-cut into a composite scene; narrow copy column.
- **Decorations:** red numbered dots that map to indexed text; product/scene cutouts rather than tape and stickers.
- **Typography:** era-aware commercial display plus typewriter/utilitarian catalog copy and underlined subheads; do not copy brands.
- **Depth:** flat printed montage, not a physical scrapbook.

Best for outfits, gear, food inventories, collections, or numbered observations. Aging belongs here, but paper scraps and craft tape do not.

### 7. Clean digital calendar sticker board

Reference: `assets/style-reference-7.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** bright white digital calendar sheet placed on a clean kraft-colored backdrop.
- **Age:** contemporary and polished.
- **Color:** lively natural photographs with controlled colorful sticker accents.
- **Photos:** neatly fitted into date cells, occasional clean contour cutouts crossing grid lines.
- **Decorations:** modern flat stickers, speech bubbles, stars, labels and small digital paper notes.
- **Typography:** theme-matched month/display title plus crisp, deterministic calendar numerals and highly legible labels.
- **Depth:** light clean digital shadows; no grime, fading, wrinkles, or antique paper.

Best for monthly recaps, habits, meals, outfits, pets, or learning logs. Build dates deterministically.

Must avoid: yellowed calendar paper, distressed textures, random dates, heavy analog tape.

### 8. Bold marker-and-cutout zine diary

Reference: `assets/style-reference-9.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** flat pale printed worksheet or form paper with faint column labels; not a thick antique notebook.
- **Age:** youthful handmade/zine energy; light analog imperfection without overall sepia aging.
- **Color:** bold opaque marker or paint blocks—red, yellow and cyan in the reference, dynamically adapted when requested while retaining strong graphic contrast.
- **Photos:** clean contour-cut people and objects pasted directly onto the flat page; a few rectangular source images.
- **Decorations:** broad marker swatches, underlines, stars, simple arrows, a few beige tape strips.
- **Typography:** large hand-painted title whose stroke energy and construction follow the subject, plus dense but intentional handwritten commentary.
- **Depth:** primarily flat, screen-printed or photocopied zine feeling; minimal cast shadows.

Best for reviews, personal interests, concerts, school memories, food, or expressive travel diaries.

Must avoid: bound leather journal, fibrous cream paper everywhere, pressed-botanical scrapbook, realistic dried flowers, map fragments by default, Polaroid grids, pervasive torn edges and brown vintage wash.

### 9. Route-map memo infographic diary

Reference: `assets/style-reference-10.jpg`

Visual fingerprint:

- **Surface:** bright white or very pale blue graph/grid paper with faint geometric folds; clean and lightweight.
- **Age:** contemporary, pristine, digital-planner aesthetic.
- **Color:** navy or cobalt as the organizing color, supported by pale blue, small coral-red location dots, sunny yellow notes, and source-photo colors.
- **Photos:** many small clean rectangles with consistent dark-blue frames, slight rotations and minimal shadows; occasional source-derived cutout sticker may overlap a node or page edge.
- **Decorations:** dotted travel routes, transit pictograms, map pins, tickets, trains, buses, food icons, landmark line drawings and compact location cards—all in a unified flat line-art system. Prefer source-derived specific icons and silhouettes over generic travel clip art.
- **Typography:** navigational condensed header, tabular or monospaced numerals, controlled handwritten annotations, small calendar module, and short factual labels; adapt the display header to place and trip character.
- **Space:** information-rich but airy; a route connects discrete photo nodes through the full page.
- **Background enrichment:** fill inter-node regions with low-contrast source-derived layers: a 5–12% opacity coastline or street silhouette, water/leaf/sky color washes, landmark outlines, enlarged food/object line drawings, repeated micro-icons, cropped signage shapes, secondary memo cards, small photo thumbnails, and route-adjacent handwritten annotations.
- **Coverage target:** photographs and cutout stickers 35–50%; routes, icons, labels, cards, and source abstractions 30–40%; true unassigned breathing space 12–20%. Avoid a single sparse vertical chain surrounded by blank graph paper.
- **Depth:** mostly flat infographic design with only light photo elevation; no physical scrapbook simulation.

Best for day trips, itineraries, city walks, transport-heavy journeys, food trails, multi-stop travel logs, or chronological memory maps.

Must avoid: yellowed notebook paper, brown vintage wash, torn kraft layers, heavy tape, dried plants, thick shadows, realistic stamps, dense paragraph copy, unstructured photo pile.

Require user-provided or verified dates, locations, route order and transport facts when those details are shown. When facts are absent, use neutral node labels such as `STOP 01`, `WALK`, `LUNCH`, or omit the factual route copy.

## Reusable photographic treatments

Treat photographic finish as independent from page style.

### Natural contemporary

- accurate color, clean whites, moderate contrast, little visible grain
- default for glossy digital, calendar, route memo, and Japanese editorial when no film preference is given

### Fujifilm-inspired Japanese film

- soft highlight shoulder and protected bright sky detail
- subtle cyan/green shadows and cooler neutrals
- warm, believable skin with no orange cast
- slightly muted reds and yellows while greens remain nuanced
- deep but open blacks, gentle contrast and fine even grain
- occasional golden flare or halation only where supported by the source lighting
- preserve identity and do not simulate date stamps, light leaks, or dust unless requested

This treatment originates from reference 4 and can be layered onto any compatible modern style. `Fujifilm-inspired` never means yellowed paper, sepia, faded brown, torn edges, or retro ephemera.

### Documentary monochrome

- neutral or lightly warm black-and-white, preserved midtones, restrained grain
- appropriate for image archives, film strips, and secondary sequences

### Retro commercial halftone

- limited color, offset misregistration, visible print dots and flatter tonal range
- reserve for family 6 or explicit retro-ad requests

## Background enrichment from source photos

For information-rich families, derive supporting content from the actual photographs rather than filling space with generic motifs.

Extract at least three categories when visible:

1. **Color fields:** sampled sky, water, foliage, clothing, food, signage, sunset or interior lighting.
2. **Shape language:** coastlines, rails, roads, windows, branches, waves, cave openings, buildings, utensils or packaging silhouettes.
3. **Object cutouts:** people, pets, food, vehicles, instruments, plants, products, architecture and souvenirs.
4. **Texture echoes:** water ripples, leaf canopy, textiles, stone, clouds, neon, road markings or printed packaging.
5. **Information fragments:** visible signs, tickets, menu shapes, timestamps, map-like paths, categories or event labels—use actual readable facts only.

Use these at different scales:

- one large low-opacity crop or silhouette behind the main grid
- two to four medium line-art or flat-color abstractions between photo modules
- several small icons, labels, dots, patterns or micro-crops near routes and captions

Do not merely add more unrelated stickers. Background richness should echo the photos and reinforce reading flow.

## Layout structures

Apply these independently of the chosen style family.

### Single freeform page

- one continuous portrait or landscape canvas
- irregular or editorial placement according to the style family
- no central binding unless explicitly requested

### Dense two-page spread

- horizontal spread with a center seam or binding
- one dominant image, 3–5 supporting images, strong left-to-right flow
- density affects spacing and overlap only
- inherit background, edge treatment, age, typographic medium and decorations from the selected visual style; route the specific display/body voice from the subject

### Editorial grid

- stable columns, baseline alignment, clear text hierarchy
- overlaps only where allowed by the visual style

### Full-bleed photo grid

- photographs reach page edges
- paper and decoration stay minimal

### Calendar grid

- correct seven-column date structure
- photos map to actual dates

### Numbered catalog

- indexed objects and a matching information column

### Route-map flow

- one continuous dotted or solid path linking chronological photo nodes
- clear start/end or numbered stop sequence
- compact header with date/theme and optional calendar
- factual labels only when supplied

## Combining styles

Record the mix explicitly:

- `primary visual style`
- `borrowed traits` (maximum two)
- `layout structure`

Examples:

- Bold marker zine + dense two-page spread: use flat pale worksheet paper, saturated marker blocks and cutout photos across a spread; do not import old scrapbook paper.
- Japanese editorial + dense two-page spread: keep pure white stock, flat cutouts and editorial hierarchy; adapt the headline voice to the subject, then increase photo count and overlap slightly without adding tape/map/botanical clutter.
- Glossy digital scrapbook + minimal spacing: keep saturated stickers and crisp white/color fields, but reduce the number of decorations.
- Route-map memo + dense two-page spread: continue one clean blue route across the center seam, use consistent framed photo nodes, and keep the graph-paper surface pristine.

If the result could be described as “generic old notebook” but the primary fingerprint is not family 2 or 6, treat that as a style failure.
